01 · Scope

What this project is—and is not

This site documents a research effort that began as the Unified Temporal–Energetic Geometry (UTEG) framework. An independent audit identified valid quantum-mechanical relationships alongside serious dimensional and interpretive errors in the original unification claims.

Current scientific position. The characteristic quantum timescale τE = ℏ/E and the reduced Compton relationships are valid within their stated domains. They follow from established quantum mechanics and special relativity. They do not, by themselves, establish a new temporal field, a new spacetime geometry, or a theory of everything.

The purpose of this page is to preserve the useful mathematics, clearly retire invalid claims, keep negative results visible, and define what evidence would be needed before proposing new physics.

Established foundations

Quantum phase evolution, rest energy, reduced Compton wavelength, and reduced Compton time.

Open research questions

Whether a time-based reparameterization reveals independently testable structure beyond standard theory.

Historical proposals

The original Φ-field, wave-equation, nuclear-harmonic, and cosmological ideas are retained as unverified research history.

Negative evidence

The reported failure of the original universal nuclear-decay scaling prediction remains part of the record.

02 · Quantum foundation

Energy and characteristic quantum time

For a stationary energy eigenstate, quantum evolution is described by a phase factor:

Ψ(t) = Ψ(0) exp(−iEt/ℏ)

The phase is θ(t) = Et/ℏ. Defining the characteristic timescale as the time required for a one-radian phase change gives:

τE = ℏ/E

Dimensional check:

[ℏ/E] = (J·s)/J = s
Preserved relationship. τE is a characteristic phase-evolution timescale associated with a specified energy. It is not automatically a particle lifetime, the age of a system, or a universal clock period.

Important interpretation limits

  • The energy E must be specified: for example, an energy eigenvalue, a transition energy, or a rest energy.
  • Adding a constant to a Hamiltonian changes the overall quantum phase convention without changing ordinary observables. Therefore ℏ/E is not always an invariant, directly measurable clock reading.
  • For negative or zero energy references, the expression requires additional care; the simple positive-energy timescale interpretation assumes E > 0.
  • The full phase period is 2πℏ/E, not ℏ/E.

Energy–time relations

ΔE · Δt ≥ ℏ/2

This shorthand must be interpreted in a specified operational context. Time is generally an evolution parameter in ordinary quantum mechanics, not an operator universally interchangeable with position. The Mandelstam–Tamm relation connects energy spread to the time required for a specified observable or state to change appreciably.

03 · Relativistic connection

Compton time and wavelength

For a particle of invariant rest mass m, special relativity gives its rest energy:

E0 = mc²

Using this energy in the characteristic quantum timescale gives the reduced Compton time:

τC = ℏ/(mc²)

The reduced Compton wavelength is:

λ̄C = ℏ/(mc)

These yield the exact relationship:

τC = λ̄C/c
Preserve. The reduced Compton time is the reduced Compton wavelength divided by the speed of light. This is an established relationship, not evidence that a particle literally circulates around a wavelength at speed c.

Reduced time versus full phase period

TC = 2πℏ/(mc²) = 2πτC

The distinction between an angular-frequency timescale and a complete oscillation period must be maintained in all future calculations.

Domain and interpretation

These expressions apply to a massive particle's rest-energy scale. For a moving particle, total energy and rest energy must not be conflated. For massless particles, the rest-mass Compton expressions do not apply.

04 · Independent audit

Retired claims and dimensional findings

The original UTEG attempted to identify the quantum timescale ℏ/E with E/c³ and, using E = mc², with m/c. That identification fails dimensional analysis.

Why E/c³ is not time

[E/c³] = (kg·m²·s⁻²)/(m³·s⁻³) = kg·s/m

This is not the dimension of time.

Why m/c is not time

[m/c] = kg/(m·s⁻¹) = kg·s/m

This is also not the dimension of time. The identity E/c³ = m/c follows algebraically from E = mc², but it does not turn either expression into a time quantity.

The proposed 333 TeV bridge

The original equality ℏ/E = E/c³ was rearranged to produce:

E² = ℏc³

The algebraic rearrangement is formal, but the equality being rearranged is dimensionally invalid. In SI units, ℏc³ does not have dimensions of energy squared. Consequently √(ℏc³) is not an energy, and the claimed 333 TeV bridge has no valid derivation from the original equations.

Retired from the active framework: E/c³ as a time quantity; m/c as a time quantity; ℏ/E = E/c³; and the 333 TeV bridge energy. These are not presented as predictions or physical laws.

Retiring the derivation does not assert that the numerical value 333 TeV is experimentally excluded. It means the original argument does not establish it as a meaningful energy scale.

07 · Historical record

Original UTEG proposals

The following ideas appeared in the original December 2025 framework. They are retained to preserve the development history, but they are not active scientific claims.

Original temporal-potential field Φ

The original proposal defined Φ = E/(ρ₀c³), then introduced spatial derivatives and a wave-equation form. If ρ₀ is mass density, Φ has dimensions of area multiplied by time, not time. A physical field interpretation, transformation law, action, source, and boundary conditions were not established. The definition and PDE are therefore archived as unverified.

Original temporal-field Lagrangian and modified Einstein equations

The original page proposed an added temporal-field Lagrangian, a temporal stress-energy contribution, and modified gravitational equations. These expressions were not derived from a dimensionally and physically specified field model. They are archived as speculative proposals, not a viable extension of general relativity.

Original cosmological and dark-energy claims

The original page associated a temporal field with dark energy, an equation-of-state deviation, redshift signatures, and singularity resolution. No validated derivation or fit to observational data was supplied. These claims are not retained as predictions.

Original nuclear τ-harmonic proposal

The original page proposed converting nuclear levels Eₙ to τₙ=ℏ/Eₙ and looking for simple patterns. A nonlinear reparameterization can change the visual appearance of a dataset; a meaningful claim requires a specified model, a justified level index, comparison against standard nuclear structure models, uncertainty propagation, and out-of-sample predictive performance. The proposed harmonic pattern remains unverified.

Original Noether interpretation

The original page suggested that energy conservation implied a new τ-conservation law. If τ is defined as ℏ/E and E is constant, then dτ/dt=−ℏE⁻²(dE/dt)=0 follows directly by differentiation. This is a mathematical consequence of the definition, not a new Noether charge or independent conservation law. Noether's theorem must be applied to a specified action and continuous symmetry.

Original quantum equations rewritten in τ notation

Substituting m=ℏ/(c²τ) into an existing equation is a change of variables or parameterization, provided the assumptions are valid. It does not establish that τ is a new independent physical degree of freedom. The standard Schrödinger and Dirac equations remain the governing equations in their usual formulations.

Historical acknowledgement and correspondence

The original page reported that Michael Shara reviewed the expressions E/c³ and m/c in August 2025 and described them as dimensionally consistent. This website does not independently verify the correspondence or its precise context. The present audit's dimensional calculation shows that, in SI units, neither expression has dimensions of time. The historical report should not be represented as endorsement of UTEG or as validation of those expressions.

08 · Research program

What would move the research forward?

The surviving question is narrower and more defensible:

Can a physically meaningful relationship between quantum characteristic timescales and independently measurable phenomena be established beyond what standard quantum mechanics and relativity already predict?

Research sequence

  1. Fix definitions. Specify the physical energy used in every τ=ℏ/E calculation and distinguish rest, transition, and eigenstate energies.
  2. Reproduce the negative test. Publish the original nuclear-decay dataset, analysis, code, exclusions, uncertainties, and protocol so the reported failure can be independently checked.
  3. Establish a null model. Compare any proposed τ-space pattern against the standard energy-space description and relevant established nuclear or atomic models.
  4. Pre-register a new hypothesis. Specify the observable, sample selection, mathematical prediction, uncertainty model, and falsification criterion before examining confirmatory data.
  5. Require new explanatory or predictive value. A change of variables alone is not new physics. A candidate result must improve prediction, reveal an invariant structure, or produce a distinct measurable consequence.
  6. Only then consider new dynamics. A temporal field would require a well-defined field variable, units, symmetries, covariant dynamics, coupling to known matter, conservation laws, and experimental constraints.
Research discipline. Do not invent a replacement equation to repair a failed one. New equations should enter the framework only after their variables, units, assumptions, derivation, and testable consequences have been independently justified.

Possible future domains include nuclear spectroscopy and precision atomic measurements. These are research directions, not current UTEG predictions.

09 · Illumina cycle

A live lunar rhythm for the research

TIME follows alternating astronomical Full Moons. Each designated Full Moon launches a new Illumina cycle: 42 calendar days of active research, followed by a rest and integration interval that lasts until the next designated Full Moon.

Calendar rules. The original Illumina 1 Full Moon on 5 November 2025 is the fixed anchor. Every second Full Moon after that is a launch. Days 1–42 are active; the remaining days before the next launch are rest days. The break is not forced to exactly 17 days.

Full Moon instants are calculated in the browser using the periodic corrections in Jean Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms, 2nd edition, Chapter 49. The calculation produces a dynamical-time (TT) Julian date; the page subtracts an estimated ΔT = TT − UT1 to obtain an approximate UTC instant, then displays that same instant in the visitor’s local time zone. UT1 is treated as UTC to within the sub-second range relevant to this calendar.

No API, network request, external library, or cached lunar dataset is required. The embedded ΔT table uses annual IERS/USNO values through 2025 and a documented 0.15-second-per-year extrapolation beyond the table. Future ΔT is uncertain, so displayed future instants are astronomical estimates, not second-level guarantees. The Full Moon is a global geocentric event; only its displayed clock time and calendar date vary by time zone.

References: USNO · ΔT data · IERS Bulletin A · Earth orientation · Meeus Chapter 49 reference implementation.

How each Illumina cycle is used

  1. Days 1–7 · Orientation: define the question, gather primary literature, and write down assumptions.
  2. Days 8–21 · Deep work: derive, compare, and challenge the mathematical structure.
  3. Days 22–35 · Testing: run analytical checks, simulations, and comparisons with established results.
  4. Days 36–42 · Synthesis: write the report, update the equation register, document failures, and decide what comes next.
  5. Rest interval · Integration: step away from active research; preserve notes and prepare the next cycle without forcing progress.
10 · Multi-cycle research program

Nine stages toward a defensible theory of time

This is a proposed four-to-six-month program, paced by Illumina cycles. The order is deliberate: establish the foundations before proposing new dynamics, and do not move to experiments until a genuinely novel quantitative prediction survives theoretical scrutiny.

Central research question. Can a more fundamental description of time be derived that is mathematically coherent, recovers established physics in its tested domains, and yields at least one distinct, falsifiable prediction?

Working hypothesis: investigate relational descriptions of time and spacetime as the primary conceptual direction. Treat the Riemann zeta function, spectral statistics, and random-matrix theory as a supporting mathematical branch only. No physical connection is assumed.

Stage 1 · Reconstruct and audit the original UTEG proposal

Timing: Illumina 7 completion / first research milestone.

Objective: create a complete, traceable inventory of original claims, equations, definitions, assumptions, predictions, and reported tests.

Work: dimensional analysis; derive every equation from stated premises; separate definitions from laws; identify circular reasoning; preserve negative results and historical versions.

Deliverables: audited equation register, claim-by-claim status table, reproducibility checklist for the nuclear-decay scaling test, and a concise audit report.

Advance gate: every surviving equation has defined variables, units, assumptions, and a derivation—or is explicitly marked conjectural.

Research prompt: “Audit the original UTEG framework as a skeptical mathematical physicist. Do not repair an equation by guessing. For each claim, state its premises, derive it step by step, check dimensions and limiting cases, compare it with established theory, and classify it as valid, redundant, unsupported, contradicted, or unresolved. Preserve failures. Identify the smallest set of legitimate open questions.”

Stage 2 · Map the modern theories of time

Objective: understand how time is treated in classical mechanics, special and general relativity, quantum mechanics, quantum field theory, thermodynamics, and quantum gravity.

Work: distinguish coordinate time, proper time, parameter time, relational time, emergent time, and thermodynamic arrows; map major approaches and their open problems.

Deliverables: annotated literature review, concept map, glossary, and a table of unresolved problems.

Advance gate: identify a specific gap not already answered by an established framework.

Research prompt: “Prepare a rigorous literature map of the physical concept of time. Prioritize primary papers and authoritative reviews. Compare the roles of time in Newtonian mechanics, relativity, quantum theory, thermodynamics, and quantum-gravity programs. Separate consensus from active debate. Identify open problems and explain what a new model would need to add.”

Stage 3 · Establish the mathematical reference framework

Objective: formalize the standard structures any candidate model must recover.

Work: quantum phase evolution; energy eigenstates and characteristic times; energy-time relations and their operational meanings; four-vectors, proper time, Lorentz invariance, and the metric; action principles and symmetries.

Deliverables: derivation notebook, notation standard, units table, and reference equations.

Advance gate: reproduce the standard results without changing their physical meaning.

Research prompt: “Build a self-contained mathematical reference for time in quantum mechanics and relativity. Derive each equation from standard postulates or established results. Clarify the interpretation and limitations of τ=ℏ/E, Compton time, proper time, and energy-time uncertainty. Do not treat a characteristic timescale as a new time dimension without evidence.”

Stage 4 · Develop a relational temporal model

Objective: formulate the primary speculative direction without presuming it is correct.

Work: define what physical systems or events are related; specify observables and state variables; decide whether time is fundamental, relational, emergent, or merely a parameter; propose a minimal mathematical structure and compare it with existing relational approaches.

Deliverables: candidate axioms, definitions, toy model, and explicit list of assumptions.

Advance gate: the model is unambiguous and is not just a relabeling of standard time.

Research prompt: “Explore whether a relational description of temporal order or duration can be formulated consistently. Begin with the smallest possible model. Define its entities, observables, mathematical objects, and operational meaning. Compare it to existing relational-time approaches. Try to show that the proposal is redundant, inconsistent, or empirically empty before developing it further.”

Stage 5 · Recover known quantum mechanics

Objective: determine whether the candidate framework can reproduce standard quantum predictions in the regimes where they are established.

Work: recover unitary evolution, the Schrödinger equation, stationary states, transition probabilities, and appropriate conservation laws; identify any new assumptions or degrees of freedom.

Deliverables: explicit recovery derivations, worked examples, and counterexamples.

Advance gate: no disagreement with established quantum results without a clearly defined and experimentally excluded or supported domain.

Research prompt: “Test whether the proposed relational temporal model can recover standard quantum mechanics. Derive, do not assert, the relevant equations and predictions. Check simple systems first. Identify exactly where the model matches, differs, or fails. If it only changes notation, state that plainly.”

Stage 6 · Test relativistic and gravitational compatibility

Objective: establish whether the candidate is compatible with Lorentz symmetry and the tested structure of general relativity.

Work: transformation properties, causal structure, proper time, covariance, equivalence principle constraints, conservation, and coupling to matter/fields if applicable.

Deliverables: covariance analysis, limiting-case checks, and a compatibility report.

Advance gate: no preferred frame or violation of established constraints unless the model predicts a quantitatively bounded, testable deviation.

Research prompt: “Stress-test the candidate model against special and general relativity. Identify its transformation properties, causal structure, and operational clocks. Check Lorentz invariance, covariance, proper-time behavior, and known experimental constraints. Derive the Newtonian and weak-field limits where relevant. Reject unsupported claims of unification.”

Stage 7 · Investigate spectra, zeta functions, and random matrices

Objective: investigate whether spectral mathematics offers a useful structural analogy or a rigorously derivable connection.

Work: review spectral theory, quantum chaos, random-matrix universality, nuclear/atomic spectra, and the Riemann zeta function. Treat the Riemann hypothesis as a mathematical problem—not evidence for a temporal theory.

Deliverables: literature map, reproducible numerical notebook, null comparisons, and a statement of whether a genuine bridge exists.

Advance gate: any proposed connection must be derived and distinguishable from analogy or numerology.

Research prompt: “Examine spectral statistics and the Riemann zeta function as a possible supporting mathematical direction. Determine what is rigorously known, what is conjectural, and whether any mapping to the candidate temporal model can be derived. Compare against null models and random-matrix baselines. Do not infer a physical connection from visual similarity or numerical coincidence.”

Stage 8 · Derive novel predictions and attempt falsification

Objective: find out whether the candidate makes a prediction that standard models do not already make.

Work: define a measurable observable, parameter ranges, effect size, uncertainty budget, null hypothesis, selection rules, and preregistered falsification criteria. Begin with analytic calculations, simulations, and existing public data.

Deliverables: prediction memo, reproducible code, preregistration draft, and a go/no-go decision.

Advance gate: no new experiment is proposed until a quantitative prediction is distinct, feasible, and robust against known confounds.

Research prompt: “Act as a hostile independent reviewer. Search for a novel, quantitative prediction of the candidate framework. Compare it with the standard model and realistic null models. Estimate effect size and uncertainty. Identify confounds and conditions that would falsify the prediction. If no distinct prediction exists, recommend stopping or returning to theory.”

Stage 9 · Independent review and manuscript

Objective: produce a clear, reproducible account suitable for external criticism.

Work: write the theory and its limits; document derivations, data, code, negative findings, and unresolved questions; request independent review from relevant specialists.

Deliverables: manuscript, appendices, reproducibility package, and a record of reviewer feedback.

Advance gate: claims are proportional to evidence; the work is ready for criticism, not presented as a completed theory of everything.

Research prompt: “Write an independent-review-ready manuscript describing the research question, mathematical framework, derivations, relation to existing literature, tests, limitations, and negative results. Remove promotional language. Clearly distinguish established results, hypotheses, and open conjectures. Include enough detail for another researcher to reproduce every calculation.”

Standard instruction for every research session

Do not assume the temporal framework is correct. Attempt to disprove it.
  • Never introduce an equation without defining every symbol, checking units, and showing a derivation.
  • Distinguish mathematical consistency from physical truth.
  • Compare every proposed result with established theory and a null model.
  • Preserve negative results; do not quietly discard failed hypotheses.
  • Use theoretical work, simulations, and existing data first. Propose new experiments only after a novel prediction survives review.

Required record at the end of every stage

Each stage must leave four permanent artifacts: a research report, an equation register, a list of unresolved questions, and an explicit advance / revise / stop decision.
