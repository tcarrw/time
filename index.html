01 · Premise and approach

If a civilization could seed a universe, what would it have to do to leave a message?

TIME starts from a thought experiment, not an observation. Suppose a deep enough theory of time and energy lets a civilization survive heat death, travel across space and time, or seed new universes. It would then have a reason to leave a message in whatever it seeds. If we might one day do that, an older civilization, or an earlier universe in a chained lineage, may already have done it to ours. The program asks what such a message would have to look like, and what that requirement implies about the physics of time.

Working premise. For hypothesis construction, assume that one-way information transfer into our universe from a prior one is physically possible. This is a research premise, not an established fact. It is not a simulation hypothesis. The mechanism is left open: black holes, dark energy, a boundary or bounce, or something not yet named. The task is to ask what would have to be true for the premise to hold, and where its consequences could be observed.
Relation to SETI. Unlike SETI, which searches for signals sent through space, TIME searches for information written into the universe itself. SETI assumes sender and receiver share a channel. Here the message would sit in constants, initial conditions or topology, and would have to survive about 13.8 billion years of cosmic evolution, which makes the physics of time part of the search problem. The two searches are complementary: a null result in one says nothing about the other. TIME borrows SETI's discipline on false alarms: preregistered thresholds and independent confirmation before any claim.

Two sides, one coupling

The program attacks from two directions and does not require either to be proven first.

The message side

What could a surviving, recognizable, decodable signal look like, and how would we search for it in data that appears random? Zipf and related statistics are candidate tools, not verdicts.

Detection question

The time side

Do quantum phase, proper time, action, energy and geometry contain an unrecognized relationship that makes a new, measurable prediction?

Foundational physics question

The coupling: writable parameters. A sender can only write into what the underlying theory leaves free: dimensionless constants, initial conditions, topology. Quantities the theory fixes cannot carry a message. So a theory of time that identifies which parameters are free also identifies where to search. In the other direction, a decodable structure in those parameters would constrain what the theory can be. Progress on either side narrows the other, and neither needs to be established first.

Three problems any one-way sender must solve

The structure of the Lighthouse, Jupiter and Carrier Pigeon pages is kept here as a concept: any one-way message to an unknown receiver faces the same three problems. These are requirements of the channel, not guesses about a mind. The older sibling pages implement them with details that predate this version and may differ from current positions.

Convergent Rendezvous Principle. The Illumina page (v1.0, September 2025) proposed that independent senders facing the same mathematical and physical constraints would converge on similar conventions: short prime-length headers, compact error-correcting codes, dimensionless anchors, and optional topology flags. This is a hypothesis, not an established result. Human-designed codes converging shows shared engineering constraints, not that other senders would, and the claim that selection pressure drives the convergence still needs a mathematical model.

Acquisition · Beacon

How does a receiver notice that something is there? In the cosmic setting: a structure that is non-random and recognizable without knowing the code, sitting where randomness would otherwise be expected.

Lighthouse

Shared reference · Address and clock

How do sender and receiver agree on location and timing without shared units? The sender must anchor to dimensionless quantities or ratios of natural timescales. This role is the time question seen from the sender's side: a unit-free definition of time is exactly what a theory of time would supply.

Jupiter / ephemeris key

Durable payload · Courier

How does the content survive the gap between sending and reading, and how can a receiver verify it? Place it in the most durable carrier and frame it with synchronization, error correction and a checksum.

Carrier Pigeon / Boiled Peanuts

Self-authentication

A checksum or error-correcting code gives a pass/fail test. A random sequence passes a 16-bit check about 1 time in 65,000 per attempt, which makes false-alarm rates quantifiable once the search space is accounted for. This works only for framings we can guess; sender-agnostic searches need compressibility and entropy-based tests.

Verification principle

Frame and payload

The frame can be discoverable; the payload cannot. A good message does not need to teach its receiver a new language. Its frame, meaning the address, clock and header, can use structures the receiver would discover anyway: mathematics and dimensionless constants. But the payload must carry bits the receiver cannot predict from what it already knows. Content that is fully derivable carries no information and is indistinguishable from natural structure. This is why the carriers must be parameters the theory leaves free: only there can a payload be both unpredictable and real.

Any single sender-model is falsifiable if it predicts a specific signature. The family of all sender-models is not: no result rules out every possible sender. The program therefore aims to bound the search, as SETI does, not to refute the premise outright. Null results are expected and are results.

Zipf is a candidate detector, not a verdict. Natural processes and even random typing produce Zipf-like distributions without any sender. The target is residual structure that survives comparison with realistic null models: for example, conditional entropy across block lengths, compressibility, and decoding that passes an independent check on held-out data.

02 · Origin and working hypothesis

From the original time question to a search for inherited information

The project began by asking whether time, distance, and energy might be connected through a deeper physical relationship. The current direction adds a second question: if information could be carried into our universe from an older civilization or an earlier cosmic lineage, what traces would it leave, and could those traces reveal something about time?

The original proposed expression

T = E/c³ = m/c

The expression followed from an attempt to identify an energy- and mass-related quantity with time. In SI units, both E/c³ and m/c have dimensions kg·s/m, not seconds. Their algebraic equality follows from E = mc², but neither side is a time quantity.

Retired claim. T = E/c³ = m/c is not a valid equation for time. No replacement equation is assumed. The dimensional failure retires conclusions that depend on this identification, but it does not settle every question that motivated the original investigation.

Current working hypothesis: inherited information and contact

For research purposes, assume that contact is possible: an older civilization, an earlier universe in a cosmic lineage, or another physically connected domain may have encoded information in a way that survives into our observable universe. The encoding need not resemble ordinary language or arrive as a conventional radio transmission. It could be carried by statistical relationships, temporal patterns, correlations, boundary conditions, or other persistent physical structure.

This premise does not decide who the sender is, whether the source is biological, whether the mechanism involves black holes or daughter universes, or whether the information was intentionally sent. Those are alternative mechanisms to investigate, not assumptions required by the detection program.

Zipf as a discovery tool

Zipf's law and related rank-frequency statistics are candidate tools for searching data that appears random or unstructured. The goal is not simply to find a power law. The goal is to identify a reproducible statistical fingerprint, characterize its residual structure, and determine whether that structure supports a stable decoding procedure. Zipf-like distributions can also arise from ordinary processes, so the analysis must compare against realistic null models, preserve blind test data, and account for the full search space.

Foundational physics question

How the pieces fit. Zipf is a candidate search and fingerprinting method. Lighthouse addresses recognition, acquisition, and synchronization. Boiled Peanuts addresses message structure, error correction, decoding, and verification. TIME investigates the physical and temporal principles that could make an information pattern persist. The architecture is a research program; its success in a designed communication test would not by itself establish extraterrestrial or inter-universe contact.

Two discoveries may be related, but neither is a prerequisite for the other

  1. Information discovery: identify a reproducible, decodable structure in physical data that is not adequately explained by known processes or analysis artifacts.
  2. Temporal discovery: derive a dimensionally consistent relationship involving time, energy, phase, information, or geometry that makes a new measurable prediction.

A result in either direction can guide the other. We do not need to prove the contact hypothesis before studying temporal physics, or discover a new time equation before testing whether physical data contains recoverable information.

Other project branches

Heat Death supplies the long-term thermodynamic motivation: whether information and observers can persist as usable free-energy gradients diminish. Manifold Dynamics may provide mathematical tools for geometry or topology once its specific claims are audited. Dark Energy, Zeta, Intelligence, Illumina, Anchors, Elevator, and the other branches remain part of the project's history and supporting work; they are not presumed to demonstrate a shared mechanism.

03 · Established physics

Relationships we can safely build from

Quantum phase and energy

Ψ(t) = Ψ(0) exp(−iEt/ℏ)

For an energy eigenstate, the phase evolves at angular frequency ω = E/ℏ, relative to the chosen energy reference. A characteristic one-radian phase timescale is:

τE = ℏ/E

This is a characteristic phase scale for a specified positive energy, not automatically a lifetime, universal clock period, or directly observable absolute time. A constant shift of the Hamiltonian changes global phase without changing ordinary observables. The full 2π phase period is 2πℏ/E.

Rest energy and Compton scales

E0 = mc²
τC = ℏ/(mc²) λ̄C = ℏ/(mc)
τC = λ̄C/c

These are established scales for a massive particle. Their relation is a useful connection between a length scale and a time scale, but it does not show that time and distance are identical or that a particle literally travels around its Compton wavelength.

Action connects energy, momentum, distance, and time

S = ∫ (p · dx − H dt)

In Hamiltonian form, both p · dx and H dt have units of action (energy multiplied by time). This is a rigorous meeting point for spatial displacement and energy-time evolution. For a free massive relativistic particle, the action can also be written, with the usual sign convention, as:

S = −mc² ∫ dτ

Here τ is proper time along the worldline. These established expressions are not a derivation of a new temporal substance, but they provide a principled starting point for the original distance–energy intuition.

Higher derivatives: what the analogy does and does not say

v = dx/dt a = d²x/dt² j = d³x/dt³

The original “higher-order” idea referred to derivatives such as velocity and acceleration—not extra dimensions. We can investigate higher derivatives of dynamical quantities, phase, or action, but a derivative with respect to time presupposes a time parameter. Reversing that relationship requires a clearly defined operational clock or a relational model; it cannot be done by notation alone.

Action derivatives and elapsed time

In suitable autonomous Hamilton–Jacobi formulations, the derivative of the characteristic action with respect to energy is related to elapsed time (with endpoint and convention choices specified). This is established mechanics, not a new discovery. A research opportunity would have to identify a regime where higher-order action information leads to a new measurable prediction rather than merely restating known dynamics.

06 · What could preserve information?

Could black-hole processes, cosmic boundary conditions, topology, or daughter-universe scenarios provide a physically consistent channel? What survives, and what would be observable?

Mechanism question

Zipf is a candidate detector, not a verdict. A Zipf-like distribution by itself does not establish a sender. Natural mixtures and other ordinary processes can produce heavy-tailed or rank-frequency patterns. The target is a reproducible residual fingerprint plus a decoding result that beats strong null models and survives blind replication.

What would count as progress?

  • A preregistered statistical signature that is recovered in held-out physical data and is rare under realistic null ensembles.
  • A decoding procedure that reconstructs the same payload or invariant structure independently, with known error rates and no post-hoc tuning.
  • A temporal correlation or phase relationship that adds predictive information beyond standard models and survives instrument/systematic checks.
  • A mathematically consistent temporal model with a distinct quantitative prediction, a feasible test, and a clear falsification criterion.
  • A physically plausible transmission mechanism connecting the proposed encoding to an observable in our universe.

A statistical anomaly, successful decoding of a deliberately designed test signal, and evidence of an external sender are three different levels of result. The program must state exactly which level has been achieved.

Screening rule. Before searching a carrier, estimate its information capacity from measurement precision, cosmic variance and available data. A Shannon-style estimate over independent modes is C ≈ Σᵢ log₂(1 + sᵢ²/σᵢ²), where sᵢ is the usable signal amplitude in mode i and σᵢ is its noise, set by cosmic variance or measurement precision. A carrier that cannot hold a decodable payload is not worth searching. Then rank carriers by survival strength, capacity, available data and the quality of the null model. Boundary and crossing mechanisms, such as black holes or a cosmological bounce, are routes by which information might reach a carrier, not carriers themselves; claims about them remain contested and need their own audit.

05 · Detection and experimental program

From statistical fingerprint to synchronized decoding

The first practical branch is to build and validate the detection architecture that the contact hypothesis requires. In parallel, TIME investigates whether temporal physics offers a deeper explanation or a second way to find the same information.

Work package 0 · Zipf and information-fingerprint analysis

Build a reproducible analysis pipeline that searches a defined physical dataset for rank-frequency structure and information-bearing residuals. Begin with synthetic data and well-characterized public datasets before interpreting any astronomical anomaly as a candidate contact signal.

Interpretation boundary. Zipf-like behavior or an unusual residual is not proof of communication. A stronger result would require a stable fingerprint, a successful independent decoding procedure, and evidence that the result is not explained by ordinary physical processes or analysis artifacts.

Use the courier/beacon/address architecture to test the complete message path: framing, synchronization, payload recovery, error correction, checksum verification, and independent reconstruction. Compare proposed codes and beacon structures with established communications methods. Document the exact payload length, symbol alphabet, code rate, channel assumptions, and decoder behavior.

The engineering demonstration should establish that the architecture works when the message is deliberately encoded. A separate scientific challenge is whether any naturally occurring physical data contains a compatible, independently recoverable structure.

Work package 3 · Temporal signatures and quantum clocks

Investigate whether information-bearing patterns may appear in event intervals, phase evolution, clock correlations, or relationships between energy and proper time. Begin with established quantum phase and relativistic clock theory; look for a specific observable that adds predictive content beyond current models.

Concrete starting point. Sorci, Foo, Pikovski, Sanner, and Leibfried's 2026 Physical Review Letters paper develops a theoretical proposal for quantum signatures of proper time in trapped-ion optical clocks. It is a nearby published research direction, not an already confirmed experimental discovery. Reproduce its predictions before claiming an extension.

How the work packages connect

Zipf searches for a candidate statistical fingerprint. Lighthouse tests recognition and temporal acquisition. Boiled Peanuts tests decoding and message verification. The quantum-clock and temporal-structure program asks whether time itself supplies a deeper physical organizing principle. Success in one package does not automatically validate the others; a shared mechanism must be derived and tested.

07 · Historical record

Preserved hypotheses and negative results

The following items are retained to document the project's development. Their inclusion is not endorsement.

Original UTEG temporal potential Φ

The historical definition Φ = E/(ρ₀c³), its spatial derivatives, and proposed wave equation lacked a sufficiently specified physical field, transformation law, action, source, and boundary conditions. They remain archived as unverified proposals.

Original field Lagrangian and modified Einstein equations

The proposed temporal-field Lagrangian and stress-energy contributions were not derived from a fully defined, dimensionally consistent model. They are not active equations.

Original cosmological and dark-energy claims

The proposed temporal explanation of dark energy, additional redshift effects, and singularity resolution lacked a validated derivation and observational fit. These are historical hypotheses, not predictions.

Original nuclear τ-harmonic and universal decay scaling proposals

The original project record reports that the proposed universal scaling across nuclear decay modes was not supported. The complete dataset, code, protocol, exclusions, uncertainties, and statistical analysis still require independent inspection before describing the result as independently reproduced.

Original Noether interpretation

If τ is defined as ℏ/E and E is constant, then dτ/dt = −ℏE⁻²(dE/dt) = 0 follows directly from the definition. This is not a new Noether charge. Noether's theorem requires a specified action and continuous symmetry.

Wave–particle intuition about time

The original discussion considered whether time might have wave-like and particle-like aspects. This is preserved as a motivating analogy. Quantum systems exhibit wave–particle duality; that fact does not establish that time itself is a wave or a particle. A research version must define what is oscillating, what is discrete, how it is measured, and what differs from existing quantum-clock theory.

Connection to Illumina, anchors, and companion projects

The creative and research branches grew in the context of the original temporal hypothesis. Their historical origin should remain visible. Symbolic, musical, and organizational meaning is distinct from scientific evidence; any physical connection must be demonstrated independently.

09 · Multi-cycle research program

From contact hypothesis to a temporal test

The program has two tracks: build a rigorous method for finding and decoding possible inherited information, and independently investigate whether time has a deeper physical structure. The tracks may converge, but neither is assumed to prove the other.

Governing question. If information from an older civilization or earlier cosmic lineage could reach us, what physical signature would it leave, how could we recognize and decode it, and could the same investigation reveal a new relationship involving time?

Objective: document the initial intuition that time may emerge from a common structure involving distance and energy, and that higher-order derivatives may be relevant.

Deliverables: historical reconstruction, claim map, and corrected equation register.

Stage 3 · Reproduce the mathematical foundations

Objective: independently derive quantum phase evolution, Compton scales, relativistic proper time, action principles, and the Hamilton–Jacobi relation between action and elapsed time.

Deliverables: derivation notebook, units table, limiting-case checks, and reference equation set.

Advance gate: reproduce standard results and understand their domains and operational meanings.

Research prompt: “Derive the standard relations among energy, phase, action, spatial displacement, and proper time. State assumptions and conventions. Check units and invariance. Explain exactly what each formula measures and what it does not imply. Do not reinterpret established formulas as new physics.”

Stage 4 · Formalize the inherited-information and contact hypothesis

Objective: state what is being assumed for research purposes and enumerate physically distinct ways information might be encoded into our observable universe.

Work: distinguish deliberate communication from inherited initial conditions; consider temporal patterns, rank-frequency structure, correlations, spectra, topology, and boundary conditions. Do not assume a particular sender, mechanism, or cosmological scenario.

Deliverables: hypothesis specification, candidate signal families, mechanism map, and a list of observations that would count against the premise.

Advance gate: each candidate has an observable definition and a plausible path from encoding to detection.

Research prompt: “Assume for hypothesis construction that contact or information inheritance is physically possible. Enumerate mechanisms by which an older civilization or earlier cosmic lineage could encode information into observable physical structure. Separate intentional messages from non-intentional inherited conditions. For each mechanism, identify what survives, what we could measure, and what would falsify it. Do not present the premise as established fact.”

Stage 5 · Develop the Zipf-based fingerprint detector

Objective: determine whether Zipf and related rank-frequency statistics can identify candidate information-bearing structure in apparently random physical data.

Work: compare power-law, lognormal, mixture, and physically motivated alternatives; test residual correlations and decoding; use synthetic signals, realistic null ensembles, preregistered analysis, and held-out data.

Deliverables: reproducible statistical pipeline, null-model suite, blind-validation protocol, and decoder interface.

Advance gate: a candidate fingerprint is stable under reasonable analysis choices, rare under strong nulls, and supports independent recovery of a predefined structure.

Research prompt: “Design a rigorous Zipf-based detector for possible information embedded in physical data. Do not assume Zipf is universal or that a power law proves communication. Compare plausible distribution families, test residual information, control the look-elsewhere effect, use held-out data, and specify what successful decoding would mean.”

Stage 6 · Validate the complete Lighthouse / Boiled Peanuts chain

Objective: demonstrate that a known encoded signal can be found, synchronized, decoded, error-corrected, and independently verified under realistic noise.

Work: Lighthouse tests acquisition and synchronization; Boiled Peanuts tests framing, payload, checksum, error correction, and reconstruction. Compare against standard communications methods at equal energy and search complexity.

Deliverables: end-to-end simulator, encoder/decoder, reproducible test vectors, error-rate report, and independent implementation test.

Advance gate: an independent receiver recovers the predefined payload at the predicted error rate, or the design is revised based on the failure.

Research prompt: “Implement the Lighthouse and Boiled Peanuts concepts as an end-to-end communication benchmark. Define the signal, synchronization, header, payload, checksum, error-correction code, channel noise, and decoder. Compare with established baselines. Verify that an independent implementation recovers exactly the same payload. Keep this engineering result separate from claims of cosmic contact.”

Stage 7 · Reproduce the quantum proper-time clock proposal

Objective: independently reproduce the 2026 trapped-ion clock theory and determine the parameter regimes in which quantum corrections to proper-time dynamics could be observed.

Work: reconstruct the Hamiltonian, identify motion–clock coupling, quantify predicted phase/coherence signatures, and list experimental systematics.

Deliverables: reproducible calculation, parameter sweep, and feasibility memo.

Advance gate: calculations agree with the source paper and the experimental requirements are realistically stated.

Research prompt: “Independently reproduce Sorci et al., ‘Quantum Signatures of Proper Time in Optical Ion Clocks’ (Physical Review Letters 136, 163602, 2026). Derive the Hamiltonian and predicted observables. Reproduce figures or numerical claims where possible. Separate the paper's predictions from experimental observations. Identify parameter regimes, systematic errors, and any unaddressed testable extension.”

Stage 8 · Investigate wave-like phase and discrete clock events

Objective: turn the wave–particle intuition into precise operational questions without assuming time itself is a quantum particle.

Work: define phase evolution, clock transitions, measurement events, coherence, interference, and the role of the clock as a quantum system. Compare with established quantum clock models.

Deliverables: operational definitions and candidate observables.

Advance gate: the hypothesis says exactly what is measured and differs from standard quantum theory.

Research prompt: “Analyze whether the phrase ‘wave-like and particle-like time’ can be converted into an operationally precise hypothesis. Distinguish time as a parameter, a clock observable, phase evolution, discrete transitions, and measurement events. Map all prior work. Propose a test only if it yields a prediction not already contained in standard quantum-clock theory.”

Stage 9 · Investigate action, energy, and spatial geometry

Objective: determine whether action-based relationships or higher-order derivatives yield new temporal observables.

Work: study S=∫(p·dx−Hdt), the relativistic particle action, Hamilton–Jacobi theory, and derivatives with respect to energy. Check endpoint conventions, gauge/reference dependence, and whether any result is a reparameterization only.

Deliverables: derivation report and list of nonredundant candidate observables.

Advance gate: any candidate adds information or predictive power beyond the established formalism.

Research prompt: “Investigate action as a bridge among energy, momentum, spatial displacement, and time. Derive the relevant Hamilton–Jacobi relations and their assumptions. Explore higher derivatives only where mathematically justified. Compare each result with existing mechanics and reject mere changes of variables.”

Stage 10 · Identify a genuine research gap

Objective: choose one narrow question from either the inherited-information detection track or the temporal-physics track where the literature review shows an unresolved issue and an observable is accessible.

Work: compare competing models, identify null hypotheses, assess data and apparatus, estimate effect sizes, and consult relevant specialists or published experimental constraints.

Deliverables: research-gap memo and ranked candidate experiments.

Advance gate: novelty is documented and the test can distinguish at least two predictions.

Research prompt: “Act as a skeptical research reviewer. Find a narrowly defined unresolved question in either inherited-information detection (Zipf statistics, decoding, cosmological signatures) or temporal physics (quantum phase, proper time, action, and geometry). Search primary literature. Show what has already been done. Define the standard-model prediction, alternative prediction, observable, feasibility, and exact novelty claim. If no gap survives, recommend stopping.”

Stage 11 · Derive predictions and design the test

Objective: produce a quantitative prediction and an experiment or existing-data analysis that could falsify it.

Work: specify observables, parameters, calibration, systematics, sample selection, statistical model, uncertainty budget, preregistration, and exclusion criteria.

Deliverables: prediction memo, reproducible code, test protocol, and go/no-go decision.

Advance gate: a predicted difference is larger than or meaningfully comparable to achievable uncertainty and robust against confounds.

Research prompt: “Develop a falsifiable test for the selected hypothesis. Derive quantitative predictions under standard physics and the candidate model. Specify the observable, instrument, required precision, systematic effects, sample size or data volume, statistical test, null hypothesis, and preregistered rejection criterion. Do not proceed if the models are observationally indistinguishable.”

Stage 12 · Independent review and publication package

Objective: prepare a reproducible account that invites criticism.

Deliverables: manuscript, derivations, code, data provenance, negative results, limitations, and external-review record.

Advance gate: claims are proportional to evidence and another researcher can reproduce the work.

Research prompt: “Write an independent-review-ready report. Clearly distinguish established physics, prior literature, original hypotheses, derived results, predictions, and observations. Include reproducibility materials and negative results. Remove unsupported claims and promotional language.”

Standing instruction for every research session

Try to falsify the hypothesis before trying to extend it.
  • Never introduce an equation without defining every symbol, checking units, and deriving it.
  • Compare every proposal with the strongest relevant established model and a realistic null model.
  • Distinguish theoretical predictions from experimental observations.
  • Preserve negative results and document uncertainty.
  • End every stage with an explicit advance / revise / stop decision.

12 · Research integrity

What this record promises

TIME does not claim a completed theory. It records an evolving research program. Established results are labeled as established; hypotheses remain hypotheses; retired equations remain visible; negative results are preserved; and new claims require derivations and tests.

The contact premise may be adopted for hypothesis construction, but every empirical claim must still be evaluated against ordinary physical explanations. Each research stage must leave a research report, an equation register, a list of unresolved questions, reproducible code/data where applicable, and an explicit advance / revise / stop decision.

Historical versions should be archived rather than silently overwritten. This page is the current research direction, not a retroactive rewrite of how the project developed.
