01 · Mission and status

The experimental foundations of time

TIME is an Illumina research program investigating what physical time is, how it relates to energy and spatial geometry, and whether open questions about temporal evolution can be turned into measurable tests.

Current position. The original UTEG equation identifying time with E/c³ or m/c fails dimensional analysis and is retired as a physical time equation. That failure does not settle the broader questions that motivated the work. This version preserves the original hypothesis as history and restarts the scientific program from established physics.

Central question

Can new, experimentally distinguishable information about time be found in the relationships among quantum phase, energy, action, spatial displacement, and relativistic proper time?

Research standard

Every proposed advance must define its quantities, derive its mathematics, compare against established theory, and specify an observable that could prove it wrong.

What is established

Quantum phase evolution, action principles, relativistic proper time, Compton scales, and precision clock physics.

What remains open

Whether a particular unresolved regime or observable reveals new temporal structure beyond standard quantum mechanics and relativity.

This is a research program, not a claim that a new theory of time has been discovered. The goal is a testable result; a new equation is useful only if it adds physical content.

02 · Historical reconstruction

Where the project began

The original question was exploratory: what if time were fundamentally related to distance and energy, and what if that relationship involved a higher-order rate of change—by analogy with the progression from position to velocity to acceleration?

Reconstructed original intuition. Distance and energy might be two expressions of a more fundamental physical quantity, with time emerging from their deeper relationship. This is a historical hypothesis to investigate, not an established result.

The original proposed expression

T = E/c³ = m/c

This expression followed from the original attempt to identify an energy- and mass-related quantity with time. In SI units, both E/c³ and m/c have dimensions kg·s/m, not seconds. The algebraic identity between them follows from E = mc², but neither is a time quantity.

Retired claim. T = E/c³ = m/c is not retained as a valid equation for time. No replacement equation is assumed.

Why preserve the original question?

A failed dimensional identification invalidates conclusions that depend on that identification; it does not automatically answer every independent question that inspired it. The research task is to reconstruct the intuition precisely, compare it with existing work, and determine whether any part can be reformulated into a well-defined, testable proposition.

Historical branches

Manifold Dynamics, Dark Energy, Intelligence, Zeta, Illumina, the Elevator, and the anchor system developed in the context of the original temporal hypothesis. Their historical connection should be documented honestly. Their existence or symbolic meaning is not evidence for a physical theory. Their scientific claims will be reassessed only after the foundational research identifies a defensible connection.

03 · Established physics

Relationships we can safely build from

Quantum phase and energy

Ψ(t) = Ψ(0) exp(−iEt/ℏ)

For an energy eigenstate, the phase evolves at angular frequency ω = E/ℏ, relative to the chosen energy reference. A characteristic one-radian phase timescale is:

τE = ℏ/E

This is a characteristic phase scale for a specified positive energy, not automatically a lifetime, universal clock period, or directly observable absolute time. A constant shift of the Hamiltonian changes global phase without changing ordinary observables. The full 2π phase period is 2πℏ/E.

Rest energy and Compton scales

E0 = mc²
τC = ℏ/(mc²)       λ̄C = ℏ/(mc)
τC = λ̄C/c

These are established scales for a massive particle. Their relation is a useful connection between a length scale and a time scale, but it does not show that time and distance are identical or that a particle literally travels around its Compton wavelength.

Action connects energy, momentum, distance, and time

S = ∫ (p · dx − H dt)

In Hamiltonian form, both p · dx and H dt have units of action (energy multiplied by time). This is a rigorous meeting point for spatial displacement and energy-time evolution. For a free massive relativistic particle, the action can also be written, with the usual sign convention, as:

S = −mc² ∫ dτ

Here τ is proper time along the worldline. These established expressions are not a derivation of a new temporal substance, but they provide a principled starting point for the original distance–energy intuition.

Higher derivatives: what the analogy does and does not say

v = dx/dt    a = d²x/dt²    j = d³x/dt³

The original “higher-order” idea referred to derivatives such as velocity and acceleration—not extra dimensions. We can investigate higher derivatives of dynamical quantities, phase, or action, but a derivative with respect to time presupposes a time parameter. Reversing that relationship requires a clearly defined operational clock or a relational model; it cannot be done by notation alone.

Action derivatives and elapsed time

In suitable autonomous Hamilton–Jacobi formulations, the derivative of the characteristic action with respect to energy is related to elapsed time (with endpoint and convention choices specified). This is established mechanics, not a new discovery. A research opportunity would have to identify a regime where higher-order action information leads to a new measurable prediction rather than merely restating known dynamics.

04 · Open research questions

Questions before equations

01 · Quantum phase and proper time

How should internal quantum evolution be described when a clock's motion and gravitational environment are themselves quantum degrees of freedom?

Research frontier

02 · Wave-like and event-like time

Can we operationally distinguish continuous phase evolution from discrete clock transitions and measurement events? This is not a claim that time itself has wave-particle duality.

Conceptual question

03 · Action, energy, and displacement

Do derivatives or invariants of action encode useful temporal observables beyond established Hamilton–Jacobi and relativistic results?

Mathematical investigation

04 · Relational time

Under what conditions can temporal order or duration be defined through correlations between physical systems, and what measurements distinguish competing clock models?

Established active field

Novelty rule. “Time is relational,” “time emerges,” “time is wave-like,” and “energy is related to frequency” are not new claims by themselves. Each has substantial prior literature. Novelty must be established for a precise mechanism, result, observable, or test—not for a broad intuition.

What would count as progress?

  • A derivation that reveals a nontrivial result not already contained in a known formulation.
  • A quantitative prediction that differs from the standard model within a specified regime.
  • A new analysis of existing data that survives appropriate null models and independent checks.
  • A feasible experimental protocol with a defined observable, uncertainty budget, and falsification criterion.
05 · Experimental-first program

First candidate: quantum clocks and proper time

A promising near-term route is to study how internal clock evolution couples to quantum motion and relativistic proper time. This connects energy, phase, spatial trajectories, and time while providing observables that precision-clock experiments can access.

Why this candidate? A 2026 Physical Review Letters paper by Sorci, Foo, Pikovski, Sanner, and Leibfried develops a Hamiltonian treatment of trapped-ion clocks and predicts regimes where motion–clock entanglement and quantum corrections to proper-time dynamics could become observable. It identifies state-of-the-art trapped-ion clocks as a possible experimental platform. This is a published theoretical proposal, not a claim that the proposed effect has already been experimentally confirmed.

Proposed research question

Can an experimentally feasible trapped-ion clock protocol distinguish a fully quantum treatment of clock proper-time evolution from an effective description using a classical proper-time parameter, after known systematic effects are included?

ElementResearch specification
SystemTrapped-ion optical clock with controlled motional states; begin with published parameters and accessible regimes.
ObservableClock transition phase, coherence/visibility, and correlations or entanglement between internal clock state and motional state.
BaselineStandard relativistic time-dilation and quantum-clock Hamiltonian predictions, including known Doppler, trap, motional, and environmental corrections.
Candidate comparisonClassical proper-time parameter treatment versus a quantum treatment of motion and internal clock evolution.
FalsificationNo statistically resolvable difference at achievable precision, or complete agreement with the baseline after systematics and uncertainty are included.
First deliverableIndependent derivation and reproduction of the published model, parameter study, and a go/no-go feasibility memo. Do not claim a novel effect before the literature comparison.

Other test routes to compare

  • Quantum-clock interferometry: compare phase accumulation along paths with different proper times, with internal clock degrees of freedom treated quantum mechanically.
  • Relational-clock models: test whether conditioning on a physical clock reproduces ordinary dynamics and identify where clock imperfections change measurable predictions.
  • Action-based analysis: identify an observable whose dependence on energy derivatives of action is not already captured by standard Hamilton–Jacobi theory.
Important boundary. The 2026 trapped-ion paper already establishes a nearby research direction. Our task is to reproduce it, map the current literature, and find a genuinely unresolved extension—not to present its published predictions as our own discovery.

Dimensional audit of the retired expression

[E/c³] = [m/c] = kg·s/m ≠ s

The algebraic identity E/c³ = m/c follows from E = mc². It does not make either side a time. The former proposed “333 TeV bridge” obtained by rearranging ℏ/E = E/c³ is also retired because the equality is dimensionally invalid; the number is not established as a physical energy scale by that derivation.

07 · Historical record

Preserved hypotheses and negative results

The following items are retained to document the project's development. Their inclusion is not endorsement.

Original UTEG temporal potential Φ

The historical definition Φ = E/(ρ₀c³), its spatial derivatives, and proposed wave equation lacked a sufficiently specified physical field, transformation law, action, source, and boundary conditions. They remain archived as unverified proposals.

Original field Lagrangian and modified Einstein equations

The proposed temporal-field Lagrangian and stress-energy contributions were not derived from a fully defined, dimensionally consistent model. They are not active equations.

Original cosmological and dark-energy claims

The proposed temporal explanation of dark energy, additional redshift effects, and singularity resolution lacked a validated derivation and observational fit. These are historical hypotheses, not predictions.

Original nuclear τ-harmonic and universal decay scaling proposals

The original project record reports that the proposed universal scaling across nuclear decay modes was not supported. The complete dataset, code, protocol, exclusions, uncertainties, and statistical analysis still require independent inspection before describing the result as independently reproduced.

Original Noether interpretation

If τ is defined as ℏ/E and E is constant, then dτ/dt = −ℏE⁻²(dE/dt) = 0 follows directly from the definition. This is not a new Noether charge. Noether's theorem requires a specified action and continuous symmetry.

Wave–particle intuition about time

The original discussion considered whether time might have wave-like and particle-like aspects. This is preserved as a motivating analogy. Quantum systems exhibit wave–particle duality; that fact does not establish that time itself is a wave or a particle. A research version must define what is oscillating, what is discrete, how it is measured, and what differs from existing quantum-clock theory.

Connection to Illumina, anchors, and companion projects

The creative and research branches grew in the context of the original temporal hypothesis. Their historical origin should remain visible. Symbolic, musical, and organizational meaning is distinct from scientific evidence; any physical connection must be demonstrated independently.

09 · Illumina cycle

A live lunar rhythm for the research

TIME follows alternating astronomical Full Moons. Each designated Full Moon launches a new Illumina cycle: 42 calendar days of active research, followed by a rest and integration interval that lasts until the next designated Full Moon.

Calendar rules. The original Illumina 1 Full Moon on 5 November 2025 is the fixed anchor. Every second Full Moon after that is a launch. Days 1–42 are active; the remaining days before the next launch are rest days. The break is not forced to exactly 17 days.

Full Moon instants are calculated in the browser using the periodic corrections in Jean Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms, 2nd edition, Chapter 49. The calculation produces a dynamical-time (TT) Julian date; the page subtracts an estimated ΔT = TT − UT1 to obtain an approximate UTC instant, then displays that same instant in the visitor’s local time zone. UT1 is treated as UTC to within the sub-second range relevant to this calendar.

No API, network request, external library, or cached lunar dataset is required. The embedded ΔT table uses annual IERS/USNO values through 2025 and a documented 0.15-second-per-year extrapolation beyond the table. Future ΔT is uncertain, so displayed future instants are astronomical estimates, not second-level guarantees. The Full Moon is a global geocentric event; only its displayed clock time and calendar date vary by time zone.

References: USNO · ΔT data · IERS Bulletin A · Earth orientation · Meeus Chapter 49 reference implementation.

How each Illumina cycle is used

  1. Days 1–7 · Orientation: define the question, gather primary literature, and write down assumptions.
  2. Days 8–21 · Deep work: derive, compare, and challenge the mathematical structure.
  3. Days 22–35 · Testing: run analytical checks, simulations, and comparisons with established results.
  4. Days 36–42 · Synthesis: write the report, update the equation register, document failures, and decide what comes next.
  5. Rest interval · Integration: step away from active research; preserve notes and prepare the next cycle without forcing progress.
09 · Multi-cycle research program

From original intuition to a test

The sequence is deliberately experimental-first. We begin by reconstructing and learning, then reproduce existing results, locate a genuine gap, and only then propose a new test.

Governing question. Can a precise, physically meaningful relationship involving temporal evolution, energy, action, and spatial geometry produce an observable result that is not already predicted by established physics?
Stage 1 · Reconstruct the original hypothesis

Objective: document the initial intuition that time may emerge from a common structure involving distance and energy, and that higher-order derivatives may be relevant.

Work: reconstruct the derivation of T=E/c³=m/c; identify each assumption and transformation; separate the original idea from later theoretical branches; preserve the dimensional failure.

Deliverables: historical reconstruction, claim map, and corrected equation register.

Advance gate: no ambiguity about what was originally proposed and which conclusions depended on it.

Research prompt: “Reconstruct the original temporal hypothesis from the project record. Do not repair the original equation by guessing. Explain the intended relationship among distance, energy, time, and higher-order derivatives. Identify every mathematical step, hidden assumption, dimensional issue, and downstream claim. Separate historical motivation from established physics.”

Stage 2 · Map established theories and prior work

Objective: identify what is already known about time in relativity, quantum mechanics, quantum clocks, relational time, thermodynamics, and quantum gravity.

Work: prioritize primary papers and authoritative reviews; include Page–Wootters, thermal time, quantum-clock theory, action principles, and recent proper-time interferometry proposals.

Deliverables: annotated literature map, terminology glossary, and explicit list of open problems.

Advance gate: identify a specific question not already answered by an existing paper or experiment.

Research prompt: “Prepare a rigorous literature review of operational and theoretical approaches to time. Compare standard clock time, proper time, quantum phase, relational time, thermal time, and emergent-time proposals. For each, identify assumptions, established results, unresolved issues, and experiments. Do not call a broad existing idea novel.”

Stage 3 · Reproduce the mathematical foundations

Objective: independently derive quantum phase evolution, Compton scales, relativistic proper time, action principles, and the Hamilton–Jacobi relation between action and elapsed time.

Deliverables: derivation notebook, units table, limiting-case checks, and reference equation set.

Advance gate: reproduce standard results and understand their domains and operational meanings.

Research prompt: “Derive the standard relations among energy, phase, action, spatial displacement, and proper time. State assumptions and conventions. Check units and invariance. Explain exactly what each formula measures and what it does not imply. Do not reinterpret established formulas as new physics.”

Stage 4 · Reproduce the quantum proper-time clock proposal

Objective: independently reproduce the 2026 trapped-ion clock theory and determine the parameter regimes in which quantum corrections to proper-time dynamics could be observed.

Work: reconstruct the Hamiltonian, identify motion–clock coupling, quantify predicted phase/coherence signatures, and list experimental systematics.

Deliverables: reproducible calculation, parameter sweep, and feasibility memo.

Advance gate: calculations agree with the source paper and the experimental requirements are realistically stated.

Research prompt: “Independently reproduce Sorci et al., ‘Quantum Signatures of Proper Time in Optical Ion Clocks’ (Physical Review Letters 136, 163602, 2026). Derive the Hamiltonian and predicted observables. Reproduce figures or numerical claims where possible. Separate the paper's predictions from experimental observations. Identify parameter regimes, systematic errors, and any unaddressed testable extension.”

Stage 5 · Investigate wave-like phase and discrete clock events

Objective: turn the wave–particle intuition into precise operational questions without assuming time itself is a quantum particle.

Work: define phase evolution, clock transitions, measurement events, coherence, interference, and the role of the clock as a quantum system. Compare with established quantum clock models.

Deliverables: operational definitions and candidate observables.

Advance gate: the hypothesis says exactly what is measured and differs from standard quantum theory.

Research prompt: “Analyze whether the phrase ‘wave-like and particle-like time’ can be converted into an operationally precise hypothesis. Distinguish time as a parameter, a clock observable, phase evolution, discrete transitions, and measurement events. Map all prior work. Propose a test only if it yields a prediction not already contained in standard quantum-clock theory.”

Stage 6 · Investigate action, energy, and spatial geometry

Objective: determine whether action-based relationships or higher-order derivatives yield new temporal observables.

Work: study S=∫(p·dx−Hdt), the relativistic particle action, Hamilton–Jacobi theory, and derivatives with respect to energy. Check endpoint conventions, gauge/reference dependence, and whether any result is a reparameterization only.

Deliverables: derivation report and list of nonredundant candidate observables.

Advance gate: any candidate adds information or predictive power beyond the established formalism.

Research prompt: “Investigate action as a bridge among energy, momentum, spatial displacement, and time. Derive the relevant Hamilton–Jacobi relations and their assumptions. Explore higher derivatives only where mathematically justified. Compare each result with existing mechanics and reject mere changes of variables.”

Stage 7 · Identify a genuine research gap

Objective: choose one narrow question where the literature review shows an unresolved issue and an observable is accessible.

Work: compare competing models, identify null hypotheses, assess data and apparatus, estimate effect sizes, and consult relevant specialists or published experimental constraints.

Deliverables: research-gap memo and ranked candidate experiments.

Advance gate: novelty is documented and the test can distinguish at least two predictions.

Research prompt: “Act as a skeptical research reviewer. Find a narrowly defined unresolved question at the intersection of quantum phase, proper time, action, and spatial geometry. Search primary literature. Show what has already been done. Define the standard-model prediction, alternative prediction, observable, feasibility, and exact novelty claim. If no gap survives, recommend stopping.”

Stage 8 · Derive predictions and design the test

Objective: produce a quantitative prediction and an experiment or existing-data analysis that could falsify it.

Work: specify observables, parameters, calibration, systematics, sample selection, statistical model, uncertainty budget, preregistration, and exclusion criteria.

Deliverables: prediction memo, reproducible code, test protocol, and go/no-go decision.

Advance gate: a predicted difference is larger than or meaningfully comparable to achievable uncertainty and robust against confounds.

Research prompt: “Develop a falsifiable test for the selected hypothesis. Derive quantitative predictions under standard physics and the candidate model. Specify the observable, instrument, required precision, systematic effects, sample size or data volume, statistical test, null hypothesis, and preregistered rejection criterion. Do not proceed if the models are observationally indistinguishable.”

Stage 9 · Independent review and publication package

Objective: prepare a reproducible account that invites criticism.

Deliverables: manuscript, derivations, code, data provenance, negative results, limitations, and external-review record.

Advance gate: claims are proportional to evidence and another researcher can reproduce the work.

Research prompt: “Write an independent-review-ready report. Clearly distinguish established physics, prior literature, original hypotheses, derived results, predictions, and observations. Include reproducibility materials and negative results. Remove unsupported claims and promotional language.”

Standing instruction for every research session

Try to falsify the hypothesis before trying to extend it.
  • Never introduce an equation without defining every symbol, checking units, and deriving it.
  • Compare every proposal with the strongest relevant established model and a realistic null model.
  • Distinguish theoretical predictions from experimental observations.
  • Preserve negative results and document uncertainty.
  • End every stage with an explicit advance / revise / stop decision.

12 · Research integrity

What this record promises

TIME does not claim a completed theory. It records an evolving research program. Established results are labeled as established; hypotheses remain hypotheses; retired equations remain visible; negative results are preserved; and new claims require derivations and tests.

Each research stage must leave four permanent artifacts: a research report, an equation register, a list of unresolved questions, and an explicit advance / revise / stop decision.

Historical versions should be archived rather than silently overwritten. This page is the current research direction, not a retroactive rewrite of how the project developed.
